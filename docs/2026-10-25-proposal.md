# WorldWar: unified modernization and protest research proposal

Prepared October 25, 2026 by the independent third reviewer. This is a project direction for discussion, not an approved engineering plan, implementation, selected vendor, spending commitment, or production change.

**Recommendation: develop one substantial research application with two connected subject areas—Conflicts and Protests—on a shared, durable data platform. Modernize the entire WorldWar experience and its existing data, while introducing a distinct protest observation and analysis model.** Preserve the current product’s central strength: understanding how actors and conflicts connect. Give the new protest layer its own factual foundation, starting September 1, 2026, and investigate relationships only after establishing what occurred.

This is a justified replacement of the present application structure, with selective migration of content and useful behavior. It should have a working backend, conventional SQL database, documented research access, editorial maintenance, coordinated geographic/time exploration, and a professionally designed interface. A second page attached to the current script would undershoot the stated ambition. Conversely, a proliferation of services, an arbitrary index, or a spectacular map would not establish completeness.

## How the two earlier reports should be reconciled

Their central conclusions converge once the later user clarification controls: a conventional research store, appropriate domain models, source provenance, independent analytical access, a complete interface, and maintainable operations are all necessary. I agree with the expanded architecture recommendation, not the original narrow “another view needs no rewrite” framing as a prescription for this project.

The material refinements are:

1. **Make the project unified in both product and data terms.** Shared branding, navigation, country/place context, source identities, search, methodology, and maintenance should make this one application. Conflicts and protests remain distinct investigative lenses. They should not be two disconnected sites or two incompatible databases.
2. **Do not describe the current product as a news feed.** Its actual map interactions and About content confirm that editorial conflict relationships are central. Chronological developments deserve a better implementation, but the supporting article feed is not the whole product.
3. **Evidence remediation is a larger migration concern than the earlier source counts suggest.** None of the current populated citations is a direct source link. Existing “High” confidence is an inherited editorial label, not a demonstrated evidence standard. Preserve it as legacy metadata, then review it under the new standard.
4. **The time problem is semantic too.** Ingestion assigns GDELT’s `seendate` to a field named `published_at`; its normalizer handles a fourteen-digit form but passes the observed compact `T…Z` form through. All 64 timestamps failed the JavaScript date parser used in this review, and Safari displays the raw strings. Migration must retain what the provider actually supplied and separate discovery time, publisher date, and event date; prettifying a mislabeled date is insufficient.
5. **Occurrences and campaigns are useful; a compulsory “wave” layer is premature.** Publish a supported occurrence before its campaign membership is settled. Allow campaign membership to change with evidence. Treat waves initially as saved analytical groupings/findings, with explicit definitions and versions, rather than requiring every occurrence to belong to a presumed global uprising.
6. **Relationships should be grounded in actors/campaigns, not automatically in countries.** A movement’s solidarity statement can relate two campaigns. A shared country or issue is a comparison dimension. Similar timing should not produce a causal arrow.
7. **Political context remains a product requirement whose depth is staged.** Basic dated government/institutional context belongs in the complete initial experience. More ambitious ideology, political direction, and public-opinion comparison need their own evidence and methodology; calling the entire companion optional risks dropping the user’s intent.
8. **Source-independent architecture is feasible; sustained worldwide observation is not yet demonstrated.** Neither report proves September backfill completeness, multilingual coverage, available editorial capacity, or rights to distribute every desired source. These must become explicit validation deliverables, not assumptions hidden behind the framework choice.

## The product we should build

The product should help a reader answer: **what is happening, where, how it has developed, who is involved, what connects it to other activity, and what evidence supports that understanding?** It should let the owner answer those questions directly against the underlying data without scraping the interface.

Use one application shell with provisional navigation **Overview · Conflicts · Protests · Data & Methodology**, plus global search. These are working labels, not a demand to rename WorldWar. “Protests” is the more neutral operational label; “uprising” can describe a documented characterization or defined subset. The public promise should be a researched observatory of significant activity with explicit coverage, not a claim to capture every event on Earth.

The core workflows should be tangible:

- **Understand a conflict system.** Open a conflict or actor, see its involved actors and typed relationships, narrow by geography/time, inspect a relationship’s evidence, read chronological developments, and return to the same exploration state. Preserve the current ability to follow support, coercion, spillover, and escalation across conflicts.
- **Explore significant mobilization.** Choose any interval from September 1, 2026 onward; move between map, timeline, and results; open a campaign or occurrence; see participants, demands, targets, action type, response, uncertainty, and sources. An unassessed country is visibly different from a covered country with no qualifying observation.
- **Understand change.** Read an activity/momentum scorecard for a stated place or campaign and comparison period, then inspect the deduplicated observations and coverage qualifications that explain it.
- **Investigate connections.** Compare themes or timing without implying coordination; inspect published solidarity, inspiration, organizational, or other supported relationships and their exact evidence. Cross-domain links are possible when supported—for example, a campaign opposing a particular conflict—but no such connection is required for inclusion.
- **Reuse and maintain the research.** Query/export documented records with IDs and provenance; correct an occurrence, merge duplicates, revise a relationship, inspect a failed collection run, and recover the data after a failure.

Completeness means these workflows work end to end, with search, stable URLs, evidence, corrections, responsive layouts, understandable data states, and an operating maintenance path. Public accounts, comments, subscriptions, personalization, a separate graph database, and microservices are not needed to meet that promise.

For the protest inclusion policy, retain significance as a contextual judgment. Consider scale relative to local context, geographic/social spread, persistence, institutional importance, consequential disruption, and response. Require a short inclusion rationale; do not impose an arbitrary crowd threshold. Apply the policy across viewpoints. The user’s illustrative countries are not verified records.

**I recommend including significant strikes, occupations, and blockades alongside demonstrations, including qualifying institutional/corporate targets, with action types distinct. This is a recommendation for confirmation, not an already approved expansion.** A small local origin can later matter; retain candidate history internally without flooding the public map with every minor dispute. The boundary between qualifying institutional/corporate action and routine commercial or labor disputes needs examples in the coding policy.

## The shared data foundation

**Use PostgreSQL as the planning default for the operational research store.** Its SQL, relational constraints, transactions, and extensible geographic support fit entities, evidence, revisions, editorial writes, and joined queries. This is a recommendation of a conventional foundation, not a selected host or bill. SQLite is credible for a smaller single-writer collection, but is less persuasive as the default for this expanded continuously maintained web application. PostgreSQL’s documented capabilities support the fit; reliability will still depend on how the application and operations are built. [PostgreSQL capabilities](https://www.postgresql.org/about/).

“Like a BigQuery table” should translate into stable structured records, understandable columns, documented joins, reproducible queries, and useful exports. Use the operational SQL database for authoritative corrections and publication. Provide research views and versioned CSV/JSON or other appropriate exports from it. Add warehouse replication only when heavy analytics, other datasets, or a preferred research environment actually justify a second data system. A warehouse should not be a prerequisite for editing a campaign correctly.

| Shared foundation | Conflict domain | Protest domain |
|---|---|---|
| Places/geographies, explicit actor identities, source documents/publishers, claims/evidence, retrieval runs, review/revision history, publication metadata | Conflicts or theaters as first-class context; actors and their time-bounded participation/roles; typed actor/conflict relationships; dated editorial assessments and developments | Occurrences with geographic/time precision; campaigns with revisable membership; participants, targets, demands, action types, responses/outcomes; inclusion rationale |

This is a conceptual boundary, not a final schema. A country, its current government, an organization, and a movement must not collapse into one entity. A source can support multiple facts across both domains. A conflict development and a protest occurrence can share a documented link while keeping their meaning. Relationships can be stored conventionally in SQL; graph visualization does not require a graph database.

Keep at least four distinct times: occurrence/validity time, source publication time where known, retrieval/discovery time, and database review/revision time. Preserve uncertain date ranges and locations rather than manufacturing exact points. A nationwide action reported without city detail must not become an invented set of city events. Multiple stories about the same march must not become multiple marches. One story describing several substantiated events should be able to support several records.

Version important claims and analytical outputs. A correction discovered today can change the September series; users should be able to distinguish the latest corrected history from what the site had published earlier. Store publication/version references, method versions, and merge/split redirects. Keep source rights and the distinction between public fields and private review notes explicit. The private research store and a public export need not expose identical material.

The legacy migration should preserve all 82 actor records, 75 relationships, known IDs or mappings, summaries, classifications, dates, and citation text, together with the exact repository version. It should separately identify them as imported editorial assertions with inherited verification status. Recover the original “Global Conflict Systems” report if available; trace its citations rather than fabricating replacements. Preserve current article records as articles. Audit Git history for useful recoverable developments, but do not promise a complete conflict history from incidental snapshots.

The new conflict structure should avoid treating one actor’s current map category as an immutable property of the country. Its role can differ by conflict and date. Retain a derived map-display category where useful while storing the actual contextual assessments separately. The conflict timeline should distinguish documented developments, changes to editorial assessments, and newly discovered reporting. Those are different events.

## Temperature and political context

**Keep temperature scorecards, but start with intelligible observed activity and momentum rather than an unexplained 0–100 composite.** Recommend country scorecards plus campaign-level development summaries as the initial scope. The final entity/window choices remain to be agreed; a map filter must not silently redefine the official score.

Observed activity can show deduplicated occurrences, active days, locations, and campaigns. Momentum can describe whether the documented pattern is building, broadly steady, cooling, mixed, or insufficiently observed. Breadth and persistence explain those judgments. Crowd estimates, repression, arrests, injuries, concessions, and other responses are important context, but should not be silently mixed into the activity measure.

Calculations should be deterministic and inspectable. Retain their period, inputs or reproducible data version, method version, calculation time, and coverage qualifications. A late-arriving report belongs to its occurrence period. New sources, changed language coverage, missing days, syndication, and uncertain campaign grouping can alter apparent trends and must be visible. Neither missing observations nor a failed collection run means zero activity.

As of October 25, the agreed September 1 boundary yields only a short recent history. That can support carefully qualified comparisons between complete recent periods where coverage is stable; it does not establish seasonality, a normal long-term country baseline, or defensible global rankings. If comparison is not supportable, show the components and an unavailable momentum label. The scope does not authorize a global numerical temperature or a combined conflict/protest/political index.

Keep political context alongside the observations. The first complete release should provide dated governing leadership/coalition and relevant institutional context where sourced, plus attributed demands and affiliations on campaigns. Political direction or affinity should be represented through separate concepts: government/party positioning, movement demands, and representative public opinion. Any deeper comparative model comes later with its own source availability, dates, uncertainty, and definitions; the app should not infer a country’s ideology from its protests.

V-Dem is a possible institutional-context source, not an immediate public-mood or left/right gauge. Its current documentation provides country-year/country-date downloads, codebooks, and cautionary notes; it also warns against comparing absolute values across dataset releases. The current stated share-alike licensing deserves review if it is incorporated into distributable research products. No political-context dataset is selected here. [V-Dem data and documentation](https://v-dem.net/data/the-v-dem-dataset/).

## Application, interface, and operations

Recommend a **modular typed frontend, one application/data service, one relational store, and scheduled/background workers sharing the same domain rules**. This is a modular monolith with clear internal boundaries. The public app consumes published data; the protected editorial interface can inspect candidates and make traceable changes. Long ingestion/reconciliation work runs outside ordinary page requests.

Use a mainstream TypeScript framework with routing and reusable components as the frontend planning direction. Exact framework and map library should be selected after reviewing the actual page/interaction proposal. Static generation or cached pages can coexist with dynamic queries and a working backend. ECharts is not disqualified by the current prototype; its future fit should be tested against city-level data, geographic aggregation, relationship display, accessible alternatives, and performance. Reuse interaction lessons and data, not necessarily every function or current visual choice.

Favor a small managed hosting arrangement for the application, database/backups, and background work unless the user prefers to own server operations. GitHub Pages need not constrain the solution. The current workflow publishes the repository root; a replacement should publish a deliberate build output and keep private research, credentials, and backend artifacts out of public assets. Managed hosting reduces some maintenance burden but does not remove ownership of failures or recurring cost. Provider and cost selection belongs to the later plan. [Current deployment workflow](https://github.com/TappedTorso/WorldWar/blob/6dcb3feb54f9479620a29d6aa8b99193f0e882e9/.github/workflows/deploy.yml).

The visual direction should be a calm, coherent research workspace. On desktop, use an application header, a clear scope/filter area, coordinated map/results/time views, a compact selected-record preview, and an expandable or dedicated reading page. Conflicts may lead with network geography; Protests should pair a readable chronological results view with the map. Both need typed search results, persistent scope, a clear reset, and shareable detail routes.

On phones, make map/results/detail deliberate modes or tabs. Filters can open in a sheet; selection should not stack a second large overlay on top of an already dominant control panel. Evidence and chronology need comfortable text size, line length, and spacing. Color should express a limited set of meanings with text/icons as alternatives. Confidence, freshness, political context, selection, and activity should not reuse one visual signal ambiguously. Motion should be restrained. Keyboard operation, focus management, accessible names, contrast, zoom, and textual equivalents belong in the design acceptance criteria.

Treat loading, no filter matches, unassessed geography, insufficient coverage, partial collection, stale review, failed refresh, and unavailable calculation as distinct product states. Show the relevant date and reason close to the affected information. The current fresh “Events” badge beside a much older map and an undisclosed partial feed demonstrates why one generic green health indicator is inadequate.

The editorial workspace is part of the recommended complete application, even if initially designed for one maintainer: candidate queue, source attachment, occurrence reconciliation, campaign grouping, merge/split, publish/correct/retract, and job/coverage inspection. LLMs may help propose extraction or grouping; they are not sources, evidence, or the temperature calculator. Support direct manual corrections and deterministic replay of calculations.

Essential engineering quality should be tied to observable outcomes: ingestion retries do not duplicate events; invalid published records are rejected; corrections preserve provenance; exports match published versions; public users cannot read private drafts or write the database; backups can actually be restored; a failed or partial collection becomes visible; deployment can roll back; core search/map/detail/export journeys work on desktop and phone. Use appropriate automated tests and operational checks for those outcomes. This is a maintainable service, so someone must own source review, dependency updates, failed jobs, and recovery.

## Source acquisition: feasible direction, unproven coverage promise

Choose a source-independent collection design using journalism and primary institutional/movement documents as the default direction. Combine broad discovery with deliberate local/regional source lists and a multilingual review policy. Retain metadata, permitted evidence, original structured factual notes, and links; the product need not republish entire articles. Respect attribution and access/reuse terms at the source level. “Available on the web” is not a blanket export license.

GDELT DOC can supply candidate articles, but its own documentation describes article search and a maximum of 250 article-list results, not an occurrence census. Its older historical-window wording is inconsistent. I confirmed the documentation and the current repository’s partial query failure; **I did not run or prove a September backfill in this review**. Bounded, overlapping queries and source-specific discovery should be tested, including saturation, duplicate reporting, exact dates, and inaccessible articles. September history is required even if some discovery endpoints cannot supply it. [GDELT DOC documentation](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/gdelt-doc-2-0-api-debuts/).

ACLED remains a candidate only under suitable access and reuse permission. Its current FAQ distinguishes aggregated Open access, lagged Research event data, and higher-tier weekly disaggregated data. Its terms limit redistribution and say a repackaged dashboard is insufficient. Consequently it cannot be assumed to be the backbone of an independently reusable public event database. I verified those public documents; the user’s entitlement, negotiated permission, price, and intended-use compatibility remain unknown. [ACLED access](https://acleddata.com/myacled-faqs), [ACLED terms](https://acleddata.com/eula).

The honest launch goal is worldwide navigability and an explicit country/language/period coverage ledger, backed by a real process for finding significant activity. A public gap label does not excuse a permanently selective dataset presented as global. Global breadth requires source coverage and review capacity to be demonstrated and maintained. An internal bounded sample can validate the method before full backfill, but must not be advertised as the completed global history.

The most consequential feasibility test is a small, deliberately varied source exercise: several campaigns or candidate episodes across regions, languages, action types, high/low media attention, and September versus recent dates. Show source documents, occurrence reconciliation, campaign decisions, one correction/merge example, and what remains unknown. Measure the editorial effort and access failures, not merely the number of URLs collected. This is a proposed future validation deliverable, not work already performed.

## Scope status, delivery shape, and what to do next

**Established user requirements:** one substantial whole-application modernization; preservation of conflict/interconnection/chronological purpose; a new global significant-protest layer; September 1, 2026 onward with historical backfill; conventional reusable structured data for both domains; facts before connections; temperature scorecards; separately interpretable political context; full UI/UX reassessment; review and agreement before engineering planning and implementation.

**Recommendations in this proposal:** one shared application and repository, distinct domain models, PostgreSQL operational store, modular monolith/background workers, typed routed frontend, managed hosting as the default direction, a protected editorial workspace, source-independent collection, activity/momentum components before a numeric composite, basic political context in the first complete release, and significant strikes/occupations/blockades included if agreed.

**Still unresolved:** precise action/inclusion policy; public versus owner-only query/export promises; editorial owner and available capacity; acceptable refresh/review lag; operating/source budget; confidence and score methods; depth of political interpretation; exact frontend/map/hosting choices; branding. These should be resolved at the right level, not all pushed back to the user as technology homework.

Deliver this as stages of the same durable product:

1. **Align on the product direction and evidence policy.** Discuss this unified proposal, settle the few consequential scope choices, and agree what a complete first release must do. No implementation begins from this review alone.
2. **Produce a decision-ready design and feasibility packet during the subsequent planning stage.** This includes concrete desktop/mobile visual proposals for both domains; an annotated discover → select → investigate → return flow; a detailed evidence page and scorecard; degraded/empty/coverage states; a conceptual data dictionary with legacy mappings; the bounded source/backfill proof; candidate score methods tested on illustrative reconciled data; and a compact architecture/hosting cost comparison with operating ownership. These artifacts make the eventual execution plan reviewable. They are not permission to deploy or sign up for services.
3. **Build and release the complete application in coherent increments after approval.** Establish the shared data/editorial foundation, migrate WorldWar without losing its content/relationships, bring the modernized public application and new protest workflow together, complete the agreed historical collection/coverage review, and validate the end-to-end product. Keep the old public experience available until the replacement satisfies its acceptance criteria. These are delivery outcomes, not a detailed task schedule.
4. **Expand analytical depth after the foundation works.** Add better supported relationship findings, richer political-direction/public-opinion context, longer-baseline methods, and warehouse integration if useful. This is continued growth of the same application; the essential workflows, history, evidence, scorecards, and basic context must not be deferred into an indefinite “later.”

The next discussion should concentrate on four choices:

- **Action scope:** accept the recommended wider significant collective-mobilization scope, including strikes, occupations, blockades, and qualifying corporate/institutional targets, or narrow it deliberately.
- **Research audience:** guarantee full querying/export for the owner; decide whether a rights-aware public export/API is also a first-release promise. I recommend building for public-compatible structured outputs while keeping internal review material private, but not promising unrestricted redistribution before the source review.
- **Operating commitment:** identify who reviews/publishes and the acceptable freshness/maintenance budget. I recommend an explicitly reviewed research cadence over an unsupported “live everywhere” promise; the exact lag should follow source tests and available capacity.
- **Interpretive depth:** include dated factual political context now; agree that political direction/affinity analysis and any numerical temperature composite require a separate method review. This preserves the requested feature without pretending its method is already settled.

**What we should do next is approve or revise this unified product direction, then commission the concrete design/source/data-method packet and architecture decision as part of the separate execution-planning stage.** Do not start by attaching a protest page to the current site, selecting a fashionable stack, or choosing score weights. The main uncertainty is whether the proposed observation and review process can support the product’s global historical claims; the main design decision is how one coherent application makes both conflict networks and protest development understandable. Resolve those with reviewable examples, then turn the agreed direction into engineering work.
